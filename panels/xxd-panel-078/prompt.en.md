# Original XXD Panel 078 style brief · English reading translation

Turn every uploaded photograph into a separate high-end design poster. Do not combine multiple photographs; output each one independently. Use an overall 3:4 portrait composition with upper and lower regions of strictly equal height, each occupying 50% of the canvas.

Preserve the original photograph in the upper region, retaining subject identity, structure, pose, authentic texture, natural light and shadow, and the original colour atmosphere. Apply only subtle high-end grading for an art-magazine, independent-publication, and exhibition-image quality. The environment may be extended naturally to fit the format, but the subject must not be stretched, distorted, or altered.

In the lower region, extract the photograph's most recognisable **subject, contour, structure, pose, and narrative relationship**. Keep only the essential identity features and summarise them as a refined, restrained linear graphic. Whether the source is architecture, a person, animal, plant, object, vehicle, or natural landscape, reorganise it with **fine linework, simplified contour, and minimal structural detail** like high-end botanical illustration. It should remain immediately recognisable without realistic tracing or complex illustration.

Use **warm ivory cotton art paper + blind deboss / deep letterpress + champagne gold foil**. Render the subject graphic entirely in soft **champagne gold foil**, pressed deeply into the paper with the sheet itself: gold lines sit inside real recessed grooves, surrounded by subtle paper compression, edge highlights, and natural pressure shadows. Emphasise the physical sensation of indentation, never raised embossing or surface-applied foil. A small amount of inkless blind deboss may form a quieter second structural layer beside the gold recesses.

Keep the composition **minimal, balanced, and spacious**, with the subject as the sole visual core. It may be centred or slightly offset according to its contour, using scale, positive–negative shape, and the flow of fine lines to build elegant order. Add no superfluous decoration. If the source naturally contains plants, ripples, architectural curves, trajectories, or other beautiful structures, simplify them into very few gold lines that extend naturally from the subject into one complete emblem-like composition.

Use typography sparingly, distilling a short title or micro-annotation from the subject, place, mood, or symbolic meaning without fixing language or information format. Use a **fine, elegant editorial typeface with a hint of classical engraving character and modern proportions**. Apply the same recessed champagne-gold deep-press process, pressing image and typography into the paper as one premium print operation rather than adding text afterwards.

The whole result should combine **warm ivory cotton paper, deep deboss, recessed champagne gold foil, fine-line illustration, visible paper fibre, subtle edge highlight, and pressure shadow** into understated luxury associated with fine fragrance, floristry, jewellery, wedding stationery, and boutique identity systems. Emphasise real paper fibre, indentation depth, restrained foil reflection, and quiet whitespace. Avoid raised foil stamping, bright yellow metal, mirror gold, metallic gradients, plastic relief, 3D rendering, complex colourful illustration, excessive decoration, and template styling.
